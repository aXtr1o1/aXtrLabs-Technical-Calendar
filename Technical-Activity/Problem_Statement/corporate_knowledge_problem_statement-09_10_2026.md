## Problem Statement

Organizations currently rely heavily on email, chat applications, and shared folders to communicate and manage development issues, deployment failures, testing problems, production incidents, infrastructure issues, security incidents, client issues, technical decisions, project discussions, HR policies, and other corporate information.

As the organization grows, information becomes scattered across different email threads, conversations, attachments, and documents. Employees may struggle to find previous issues, understand how they were resolved, identify the people involved, or determine the current status of ongoing problems.

Important discussions and technical decisions are often mixed with general conversations, making it difficult to distinguish relevant information from unrelated communication. Attachments, logs, screenshots, and supporting documents may be stored in different locations, while issue details, root causes, resolutions, and ownership information may not be consistently recorded.

Finding relevant information is also challenging when similar issues are described using different terminology. Employees may need to search through multiple communication channels to understand the history of an issue, follow its progress, or identify related discussions and documents.

Additionally, managing information across different teams, projects, and clients creates challenges in maintaining proper access restrictions. Confidential client information, HR documents, security incidents, and internal business information must remain protected from unauthorized access.

The organization also faces difficulties tracking issue assignments, status changes, approvals, important announcements, and user activities. These challenges affect collaboration, accountability, information discovery, and the effective management of technical and business knowledge.
